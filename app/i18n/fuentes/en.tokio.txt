97|Suica or PASMO
98|Tokyo's rechargeable cards: they work on the subway, JR trains, buses and even at convenience stores (konbini) and vending machines. The regular Suica is sold at machines in JR stations, with a deposit you get back when you return it.

100|A Suica for tourists, with no deposit, valid for 28 days from purchase. There is also the Welcome Suica Mobile app for your phone.
101|On your phone
102|On an iPhone you can add a Suica or PASMO to Apple Wallet and top it up with your card. Many Android phones bought outside Japan are not compatible.
103|On all Tokyo Metro and Toei lines you can go through the gate by tapping a contactless credit or debit card (or your phone with the card loaded). It charges the regular fare. It doesn't work on JR trains such as the Yamanote: for those, use Suica or PASMO.

107|Tokyo subway

144|One of the safest cities
145|Tokyo is one of the safest big cities in the world: you can walk around calmly at night and theft is rare.
146|If you lose something, it often turns up: ask at the station or at the koban, the small police box in every neighbourhood.
147|Still, keep an eye on your things in crowded places, as anywhere.
148|Bars and touts
149|In Kabukicho (Shinjuku) and Roppongi there are street touts inviting you into bars: don't follow them. There have been cases of huge bills and spiked drinks.
150|Choose bars you know or with good reviews, and check prices before ordering.
151|Many small bars charge a cover or a compulsory little starter ("otoshi"): that's normal, not a scam.
152|Earthquakes and typhoons
153|Japan has frequent tremors. If it shakes, move away from glass, protect your head and follow staff instructions.
154|Download the Safety tips app from the Japan Tourism Agency: it sends earthquake and other alerts in several languages.
155|From August to October there can be typhoons: if one is forecast, trains and flights may be suspended.
156|Documents and help
157|Carry your passport with you: the police can ask for it, and many hotels and clubs do.
158|If your passport is stolen or lost, report it at a koban and then go to your consulate: they're on the map in Info > Help.
159|Get travel insurance: medical care without insurance is expensive.
